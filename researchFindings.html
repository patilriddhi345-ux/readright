Summary

  • The study explores college students’ preferences for e-books and printed books in academic learning.
  • A mixed-method approach was used, including surveys, interviews, and observations of 44 students.
  • Most students read regularly and use both e-books and printed books for study purposes.
  • E-books are preferred for accessibility, portability, quick search features, and cost-effectiveness.
  • Printed books support better reading comfort, familiarity, and concentration.
  • The findings suggest a shift toward digital learning, with a hybrid approach being the most effective.

Key Insights

  • Students read academic books daily or several times a week.
  • Weekly reading time ranges from less than one hour to over three hours.
  • E-books are preferred for accessibility, availability, and convenience.
  • Printed books support better focus but are heavy and costly.
  • E-books may cause eye strain and digital distractions.
  • A balanced mix of e-books and printed books is recommended for libraries.
